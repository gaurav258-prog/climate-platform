02Is $90k justifiable for a once-a-year report?

The regulatory filing is annual — but the price is not paid for the filing. That distinction is the whole company.

✗ Sold as a report generator

A once-a-year form-filler is a commodity. Customers price it at the ESG-reporting median — ~$40k — and margin pressure follows. This is the failure mode, not the plan.

✓ Sold as risk intelligence

The report is the receipt. The product is continuous physical-risk intelligence that drives in-year decisions and stands up to a regulator. That earns six figures — and $90k is conservative.

Three anchors for the price — none of them is "how often they file"

01 · Money at stake

The decisions, not the document

For a €5–50bn bank, repricing climate risk on a sliver of the book beats $90k many times over. One better sourcing call ahead of a bad harvest pays for us for years. The filing is annual; the decisions run all year.

02 · Cost of getting it wrong

Priced to the downside

EUDR non-compliance: fines up to ~4% of EU turnover + goods seized. SFDR/CSRD misstatement: greenwashing enforcement, audit qualification. CSRD now needs auditor assurance. $90k to de-risk a seven-figure exposure is cheap.

03 · The alternative is pricier

Annual ≠ cheap

A Big-4 CSRD / assurance engagement runs €100–500k/yr in fees. Bank supervisory reporting (OneSumX-class) is $50–500k — much of it annual too. Nobody prices audit or disclosure on how often you file.

The reframe that settles it: value isn't login frequency. Insurance is "used" once — when you claim — yet priced on risk transferred, not clicks. We're paid for being right about the risk and letting the customer prove it. How often they file is irrelevant.

Verdict: $90k is justifiable — but it's a bet on positioning. If we ever let ourselves be sold as "the annual report button," the price collapses to ~$40k and the plan needs 2× the customers. The defence is the thing competitors lack: validated, backtested physical-risk science + continuous monitoring, with the filing as the last mile. The early-warning sign of failure: customers who only ever log in at filing season.

05What would kill it

The unit economics are not fragile; these four risks are where the plan actually lives or dies.

1 · Price compresses to commodity

If we're seen as "just another ESG reporting tool," ACV falls to $30–40k. Margin still ~80%, but we'd need ~2× the customers.

Mitigant: physical-risk + validated, backtested science is the premium wedge — reporting is the last mile, not the product.

2 · Sales cycle / CAC blows out

Financial-institution procurement runs 9–18 months. A new entrant without references can see CAC and payback double. This is the #1 real risk.

Mitigant: 1–2 lighthouse customers, land-small-then-expand, and a distribution channel (e.g. Regnology).

3 · Support balloons into consulting

If every customer needs bespoke science hand-holding, CS cost climbs and margin slides toward 70%.

Mitigant: productized outputs + the self-service data loop (upload → validate → decide → export) already built.

4 · The moat is expensive to keep

Validated climate science is a real, ongoing R&D cost — it raises the fixed base and the break-even count.

Mitigant: the same validation framework is the credibility that justifies the premium price — cost and moat are the same spend.

06What has to be true first

The ~80% we can build ourselves is done. Worthiness now hinges on the external-gated ~20% — the trust layer that unlocks six-figure FI deals.

  • 1–2 lighthouse customers — reference accounts that collapse CAC for everyone after them.
  • SOC 2 Type II / ISO 27001 — table stakes to pass FI procurement.
  • Independent / peer-reviewed validation — turns our honesty gate from posture into a third-party-verified moat.
  • Production vendor integrations + EU data residency — the last operational mile.

None of these change the unit economics above — they're the keys that let us realize them.
