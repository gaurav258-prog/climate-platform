00The rule: coverage ≠ calibration

Every channel ships with a maturity tier stamped on it. That is how we reach "all 28" honestly — some are backtested, some are disclosed indicators, and the label always says which. A channel graduates upward only when it passes the backtest, never before.

◆ TIER C — CALIBRATED

Backtested & gated

Passes the honesty gate (r²/rank skill on real outcomes). Publishes scores & € figures. E.g. drought, cyclone-severity, seismic.

◆ TIER S — SCREENING

Disclosed indicator

A real authoritative EO/agency exposure indicator, labelled "indicator, not yet calibrated." Flags risk; never dressed as skill.

◆ TIER R — REFERENCE

Zone / geophysical

Static hazard-zone or geophysical layer where a climate projection doesn't apply (or data is regional). Coverage for completeness, clearly bounded.

02The build plan

Adding a hazard is already a repeatable pattern — a HazardType entry, a data-ingest job, a scoring channel, a validator, and an Atlas row. The long pole is data acquisition for a few sources, not code. Phases are ordered by relevance × feasibility, not calendar.

Phase 0

Make coverage first-class

foundation · small

Add a maturity-tier field to every HazardType and a coverage registry keyed to the Appendix-A 28. Surface it as the “Coverage vs EU Taxonomy” panel (the Atlas view). This alone lets us honestly show all 28 — populated where we're live, roadmap where we're not — before a single new hazard is built.

High-value climate adds

Sea-level rise, water stress (upgrade), heavy precipitation, pluvial flood, landslide, land subsidence. These close the gaps a supervisor scrutinises most (water + ground movement + surface flood + coastal completion).

Phase 2

Complete acute + solid-mass chronic

6 channels · Tier S

Groundwater flood, cold-wave (extend frost), coastal erosion, soil erosion, soil degradation, saline intrusion. Many double as ESRS E3 (water) and E4 (nature) coverage.

data · satellite shoreline · ESDAC / RUSLE soil · ERA5 · regional coastal models

Phase 3

Chronic trend & variability indicators

5 channels · Tier S

Changing temperature, temperature variability, changing precipitation patterns, precipitation/hydrological variability, changing wind patterns. These are honestly indicators — reanalysis + CMIP6 trend signals, never event models. Tier S by nature.

data · ERA5 reanalysis trends · CMIP6 deltas (already in the engine)

Phase 4

Niche & geophysical tail

6 channels · Tier R

Permafrost thaw, tornado, ocean acidification, glacial-lake outburst, avalanche, solifluction. Low EU-portfolio relevance or regional; carried as reference layers, flagged "limited / regional" — completeness without over-claiming.

data · ESA CCI permafrost · lake inventories + DEM · regional catalogues (where they exist)

The two roadmaps run at different speeds — on purpose. The coverage roadmap (all 28 present, each tiered) can move fast: most new channels launch at Tier S (screening) from authoritative data. The calibration roadmap underneath is slower and honest: a channel graduates S → C only when it passes a real backtest, exactly like the 22 crop belts. So we can truthfully say "all 28 EU hazards covered" long before "all 28 backtested" — and we'll always show which is which. That gap, disclosed, is the credibility a regulator and a physical-risk PhD reward.

03What it buys us

  • Full EU-Taxonomy alignment on the completeness axis — the scoreboard a supervisor and a bank's model team judge coverage by.
  • ESRS E1/E3/E4 breadth in one engine — the water and nature channels land climate + nature disclosure together.
  • The honesty tiering is itself the differentiator — "28 covered, N calibrated, here's the ladder" beats a competitor's round number with no provenance.
  • Harder to leapfrog — a cloner can copy one channel; matching 28 tiered, sourced, and graduating channels is the compounding moat.
