Ground-Truth Fidelity

The number a critic assumes when they see “R²” is the flattering, in-sample one. We don’t report that. We report how much of independently-observed reality our model actually explains, tested out-of-sample over decades — and we give it a name of our own, so it’s never mistaken for the textbook version.

00In plain words first

Before the equations — the whole idea in everyday language.

The question every model is really answering: harvests (or losses) swing up and down every year. How much of that real swing does our model actually explain?

Compared to what? Imagine a lazy competitor who models nothing and just says “every year will be average.” That’s the bar.

  • 0 → we’re no better than that lazy “always average” guess.
  • 100 → we explain everything — every year, nailed.
  • 60 → we explain 60% of the real swing; the rest is things no model captures (pests, prices, luck).
  • negative → we’re worse than the lazy guess — so we bin it (that’s US irrigated maize).

Why ours is the strict version: the easy test lets the model peek at the answers, then draw its best line through them — of course it looks good. Ours makes the call first, then checks it against decades of real outcomes it never saw. No peeking. Much harder to pass.

So what’s “Fidelity”? It’s simply our honest name for that same number — how faithfully our model matches real life. We rename it because “R²” makes people picture the soft, peeking version, and assume we’re flattering ourselves. We’re not. Fidelity = R², the strict, tested-against-reality kind.

01The model, in mathematical statements

For one hazard model, one scope (a crop belt, a hazard region), over N paired observations — years or locations.

The paired sample
{ ( xi , yi ) } ,   i = 1 … N
xi — the model’s predicted hazard effect at unit i (e.g. the growing-season drought/heat index, oriented by its physical sign).
yi — the independently observed outcome at unit i (e.g. the real crop-yield anomaly from FAOSTAT, or the observed near-field event). This is the ground truth the model never saw.
Detrending the outcome (agri targets) — isolate the climate signal from the technology trend
ŷtrend(t) = a·t + b  (OLS over years)  →  yi = yield(ti) − ŷtrend(ti)ŷtrend(ti)
Yields rise ~1–2 %/yr from genetics & inputs; we remove that linear trend and keep the fractional residual, so yi is the climate-attributable anomaly — not the harvest total.
The fit — out-of-sample R² (Nash–Sutcliffe form)
R²oos  =  1 − SSresSStot  =  1 − Σi ( yi − xi )²Σi ( yi − ȳ )² ,   ȳ = 1N Σi yi
The numerator compares the observation to the model’s prediction directly — the predictions are not refit to this sample. That makes it the out-of-sample / Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency, stricter than the textbook in-sample R² (which would refit a best line and always flatter). It can go negative — worse than just guessing the mean ȳ — and we report that honestly, never clipped. Returns “insufficient”, not a number, when N < 3 or the outcome has no variance.
When the outcome is an event, not a continuous value — rank agreement instead of R²
ρ = corr( rank(x) , rank(y) )   ·   AUC = Σi∈pos rank(xi) − n₊(n₊+1)/2n₊ · n₋
Spearman ρ = does a higher score rank with a higher observed outcome. AUC = P( a location that had an event was scored above one that didn’t ). R² is not meaningful for occurrence data, so it is not computed — we don’t force one metric onto both.
The publish gate + grade (the honesty rule)
publish ⟺   R²oos ≥ 0.40   (continuous)   or   ρ ≥ 0.35  (event)
Grade: R² ≥ 0.60 strong · ≥ 0.40 fair · below → weak, withheld. And an applicability guard runs first: if the test physically can’t judge the model — the event target is saturated (> 85 % of places had one), too sparse (< 2 %), or the score/outcome has no spread — the run is “not testable” (insufficient), never “weak”. A sound model is never mislabelled bad because the test was blind.

02Why this isn’t the R² they think it is

Out-of-sample, not in-sample

Textbook R² refits a line to the same data and reports how well that line fits itself. Ours compares fixed predictions to unseen observations (Nash–Sutcliffe). It can be negative. It is strictly harder to pass.

Against independent ground truth

yi is real, externally-observed outcome — FAOSTAT yields, USGS events — data the model never trained on. That’s the differentiator no black-box competitor can claim.

A domain-calibrated floor

Climate is one of several drivers of any outcome, so 0.40 is a deliberate “explains a real, material share” floor — not a claim of precision. Below it we publish nothing.

Reported, not clipped

Negative and weak values are shown as weak; “insufficient” is shown as insufficient. The metric’s job is to withhold, not to flatter.

03A Tellumen name & presentation

Same statistic — presented in our terms, always with its context, so it reads as evidence, not jargon. My recommendation, with two alternatives:

Ground-Truth Fidelity ◆ recommended · symbol Φ

Names the one thing that’s ours — the fit is against independent ground truth, out-of-sample. Dignified, hard to confuse with textbook R², and it travels to the event models too (“Ranking Fidelity” for ρ/AUC under the same family).

Reality Match

Plainer and punchier for a non-technical reader; less obviously tied to the ground-truth moat.

Proven Fraction

Literal — “the fraction of real outcomes our model provably accounts for.” Strong but reads a touch marketing.

What implementing this changes, concretely: nothing about the maths — Φ is R²oos, unchanged. It’s a rename + a presentation layer: the model-validation page and the Calibration Atlas would show Φ (Ground-Truth Fidelity) with the band and the always-on context line, instead of a bare “R² = 0.60”.
